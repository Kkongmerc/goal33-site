Why systematic

Discretionary

By hand

  • The setup you skip is the one that pays
  • Size creeps up after a loss, down after a win
  • You are awake for one session out of four
  • Rules drift, and you never notice the drift
  • No sample — every trade feels like the first
Systematic

By rule

  • Every qualifying setup taken, or none — no vetoes
  • Position size is a parameter, not a mood
  • Alerts fire on the Sydney open whether or not you do
  • The rules are the rules — readable, fixed, testable
  • A published sample you can audit before you pay

Ranked by drawdown, not hype

Every system is ordered by return over max drawdown, backed by its sample size. The metric that survives contact with a real account.

Sessions, not screen time

Sydney through New York, three markets, five systems. Alerts arrive on schedule whether you are at the desk or asleep.

Sample size published

Trade counts sit next to every figure, because a profit factor on thirty trades is not the same claim as one on eight hundred. You get to tell the difference.

Automate it or trade it

Standard TradingView alerts. Route them to your own execution tooling for hands-off operation, or take the signals manually. Same system either way.

Why do you publish two windows — and why is the best window better than the full 2024+ run?

Because they answer different questions. The best window is the validated configuration run over the period it was built and tuned for — that is the engine as it ships. The full 2024+ window pushes the same rules across every market regime since January 2024, including ones the engine was never designed for, and that number is usually worse. We publish both anyway: if an edge only exists in its best stretch, the two figures side by side make that visible instead of hidden. Size and judge from the full-window numbers; treat the best window as the ceiling, not the promise. Every figure is from the validation runs — nothing smoothed, nothing cherry-picked.
